# The Northern Beach

The Northern Beach is not a welcoming place. It sits beyond a line of dark, wave-slick rocks that jut from the surf like broken teeth, cutting off easy approach from land or sea.

Those who reach it find a narrow crescent of grey sand, backed by a low cliff face and eerily quiet. The usual sounds of the island — wind through trees, distant voices, the creak of rigging — do not carry here. Whatever made its home on this beach chose isolation deliberately.

---

## Getting There

There is no easy path to the Northern Beach. The reef that guards it forces a choice:

* **Swim Around** — Cold water, strong currents, and rocks close enough to the surface that a wave in the wrong direction means torn skin. Requires a sustained Athletics check and some luck.
* **Climb Over** — The rocks are climbable but unforgiving. Handholds are plentiful and edges are razor sharp. A slip costs blood. Requires Athletics, and anyone carrying heavy gear will feel it.
* **Squeeze Through** — There are gaps in the reef where the rock formations press close together. Tight, dark, and half-submerged at high tide. Requires Acrobatics and a willingness to get scraped and soaked.

## The Lindworm Nest

The nest sits against the base of the cliff, half-sheltered by an overhang. It is unmistakably the work of something large — a great coiling of driftwood, kelp, and compacted sand, stained dark with old salt and the faint iridescent residue that clings to places where scaled things have rested for a long time.

The nest is abandoned. There are tracks leading away from it to the water. No carcasses.

At the centre of the nest, half-buried in sand and broken shell, is the remnant of a single egg. It did not go unhatched. The shell is cracked from the inside, pushed outward in the way of something that fought its way free rather than being helped. whatever hatched is long gone.

### What the Players Can Gather

* The hatchling left relatively recently — weeks, not months.
* The mother has been absent far longer. She did not abandon the egg; she was prevented from returning, or chose not to.
* The nest shows no signs of disturbance from outside. Nothing drove the hatchling off. It left on its own terms.

## The Relic Seed

Tangled in the deeper layers of the nest material — woven in with the driftwood as though it had been placed deliberately — is a single seed unlike anything growing on the island.

It is roughly the size of a clenched fist, its outer casing dark and dense, almost bark-like. It does not appear dead. When held, there is a faint warmth to it that has nothing to do with the sun.

 > 
 > **Nature or Arcana (DC 20):** This seed does not belong to any plant native to the archipelago, or to anywhere nearby. It is old. Whatever it grows into, it has been waiting a very long time.

How it came to be in the lindworm nest — whether carried here, left by the mother, or grown here slowly over years — is not immediately clear.

## The Cave

Set into the cliff face at the back of the beach, half-hidden behind a curtain of hanging kelp and accessible only at low tide without getting chest-deep in seawater, is a cave.

The entrance is low. Inside, it opens up.

The walls are smooth in the way of places that have been worn by centuries of tide. Higher up, above the waterline, there are marks — not carvings exactly, but deliberate. Patterns. Something with claws made them, carefully and repeatedly, over what must have been a very long time.

The cave goes back further than the light reaches.

## Atmosphere Notes

* The beach smells of brine, old kelp, and something faintly mineral — like hot stone after rain.
* The rocks of the reef are covered in mussels and barnacles. The footing is treacherous even on the flat sections.
* At dusk, the water between the reef rocks goes a deep, unsettling green. Something about the light, or what lies beneath.
* The silence here feels chosen, not accidental.
